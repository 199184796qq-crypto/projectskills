# Local-life shooting

Create a shootable vertical-video plan, not generic promotional copy. Center a real person, a visible task or encounter, and a specific place or service.

## Choose the format

- **Follow-shot narration:** First-person employee/customer follows a person through a task, adds comic or observational narration, and ends on a payoff. Read `references/follow-shot-narration.md`.
- **Store visit:** Use a customer problem, arrival, product/service proof, and a natural recommendation.
- **Owner story:** Use one tension between what the owner expected and what actually happens.
- **Service proof:** Show the process, evidence, and result; do not make unsupported claims.

## Core workflow

1. Identify the business, protagonist, location, audience, desired action, available real moments, and platform duration.
2. Pick one event that creates movement or tension: first visit, interview, busy period, customer request, new employee, service recovery, or challenge.
3. Write a scene-by-scene plan with time, camera action, visible action, dialogue/narration, text overlay, and each scene's function.
4. Keep claims factual. Show price, process, service, and location only when supplied or visibly verifiable.
5. End with a real payoff, question, or next-event hook rather than a hard sell.

## Default delivery

Return:

- One-line concept and hook
- A shootable 30–60 second shot list
- Narration/dialogue separated by speaker
- On-screen text suggestions
- Three optional titles

## Quality bar

- Put the person or conflict in the first three seconds.
- Make every narration line react to a visible action, expression, object, or prior line.
- Use one recurring phrase at most; repeat it only when it advances the joke or payoff.
- Avoid inventing customer reviews, discounts, sales results, or business claims.
