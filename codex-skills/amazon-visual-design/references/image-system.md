# Amazon image-system blueprint

Use this as a default structure; modify it to suit the product and marketplace.

## Listing image set (9)

1. **Main / hero** — product only, white background, precise silhouette and included components.
2. **Core outcome** — one clear customer result with a visually embodied use or result.
3. **Primary feature** — show the product mechanism, material, or construction that makes the outcome credible.
4. **Feature detail** — close-up proof of a second differentiator.
5. **How it works** — a simple 2–4 step usage flow.
6. **Use scenario** — target buyer in the highest-value everyday setting.
7. **Scale / compatibility** — dimensions, capacity, fit, or supported devices; use only confirmed specifications.
8. **Comparison / objection** — explain a defensible difference without disparaging competitors or unsupported claims.
9. **Trust / complete value** — included items, care, quality control, or another substantiated reason to choose.

## Premium A+ set (8)

1. Wide emotional brand/product banner.
2. Customer tension and desired outcome.
3. Hero benefit explained through lifestyle or controlled visual metaphor.
4. Feature mechanism / material proof.
5. Three-benefit modular panel.
6. Use-in-context panorama.
7. Decision support: dimensions, compatibility, care, or product family.
8. Closing conversion panel with brand promise and product hero.

## Brand Story set (2)

1. Brand origin, point of view, or craftsmanship signal connected to a real product benefit.
2. Brand ecosystem: a cohesive lifestyle scene or product range that invites exploration.

## Design guidance

- Choose one visual territory: clinical precision, warm domestic confidence, outdoor capability, modern minimalism, or category-appropriate equivalent.
- Keep a controlled palette: one base neutral, one brand accent, and one proof/highlight color.
- Alternate visual rhythm: packshot, detail, diagram, human context; avoid consecutive images with identical framing.
- Give each image one job. Avoid text walls and repetitive feature lists.
- Use diagrams only to clarify a real mechanism, compatibility, measurement, or process.
